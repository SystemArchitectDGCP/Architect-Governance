# System Architect DGCP™: Public Governance

**Status:** Active

**Classification:** Public Governance

**Effective Date:** 4 October 2026

**System Authority:** P’Toh: System Architect DGCP™

**Framework:** DGCP™

**License Reference:** DGCP | MMFARM-POL-2025

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the public governance framework for the System Architect DGCP™ environment.

It defines authority boundaries, repository relationships, continuity principles, public and private separation, and intellectual property stewardship without exposing the private Control Room or restricted operating architecture.

## 2. System Authority

The System Architect DGCP™ role is held by:

**P’Toh**

The System Architect may define and maintain system-level architecture, governance boundaries, repository relationships, transition controls, continuity rules, and approved structural coordination across the wider ecosystem.

This authority does not erase the functional identity of any governed unit.

## 3. Functional Independence

The following ecosystem layers may operate under distinct mandates and repositories:

- DGCP™
- MaMeeFarm™
- Groundline Research Lab
- SINTELRA Institute
- ORVESTRA
- DGCP Network

Each unit retains its own function, scope, records, and governance where applicable.

System Architect oversight does not automatically convert one unit into another or merge their records.

## 4. Repository Governance

Repositories must have a defined functional purpose.

Where a repository changes name, owner, administrative location, or functional scope, the transition should be recorded forward through traceable governance.

Historical records should remain interpretable in the context in which they were originally created.

## 5. Historical Integrity

Governance changes operate forward.

The following should not occur silently:

1. Rewriting historical records to fit a later structure.
2. Re-dating earlier work.
3. Removing provenance or authorship context.
4. Reclassifying records without a traceable governance basis.
5. Replacing an earlier repository history merely because administration changes.

Corrections and transitions should be documented through new traceable records or commits.

## 6. Public and Private Boundary

Public governance exists to make authority, relationships, integrity, and continuity understandable.

It does not require disclosure of:

- Private Control Room material
- Credentials or authentication data
- Security-sensitive configuration
- Restricted architecture
- Confidential operating instructions
- Internal reasoning
- Non-public decision records
- Protected research or client material

Public visibility must not be treated as unrestricted access to private system layers.

## 7. Intellectual Property Stewardship

The System Architect may steward original system architecture, governance structures, naming systems, repository structures, documentation, research frameworks, analytical products, publication structures, product systems, and other original assets.

Public access does not itself transfer:

- Ownership
- Governance authority
- Licensing authority
- Trademark rights
- Copyright
- Rights in confidential information
- The right to present derivative material as official

Any reuse remains subject to the applicable license, source status, third-party rights, and governing policy.

## 8. Repository Relationships

Repository ownership or administration and functional identity are separate concepts.

A repository administered through `SystemArchitectDGCP` may still belong functionally to Groundline Research Lab, MaMeeFarm™, ORVESTRA, SINTELRA Institute, DGCP™, or another governed unit.

Functional identity must be determined by the repository's declared mandate and governance records, not by account location alone.

## 9. Governance Transitions

A governance transition should identify, where applicable:

1. Effective date.
2. Repository or function affected.
3. Previous state.
4. New state.
5. Historical treatment.
6. Authority responsible.
7. Scope of the change.
8. Records or systems that remain unchanged.

Transition records must not claim a technical move has occurred unless that move has actually been completed.

## 10. IP and Global Ecosystem Positioning

The System Architect environment is governed as a structured global IP and system architecture environment rather than as a single publication channel, research unit, or website.

Public governance should preserve the ability to license, coordinate, extend, commercialize, or integrate governed assets in the future without weakening provenance or functional boundaries.

Commercial or licensing arrangements, where applicable, must be documented separately from public governance claims.

## 11. Control Room Boundary

The private System Architect Control Room remains separate from this repository.

This repository does not replace, expose, summarize, or constitute the complete internal control environment.

No public governance document should be interpreted as disclosure of the full internal system architecture or decision process.

## 12. Change Control

Changes to this governance framework should be made through traceable commits.

Substantive changes should preserve prior history and state clearly what changed.

No governance update should silently rewrite the meaning of earlier records.

## 13. Standing Governance Statement

**Visible authority. Preserved history. Controlled boundaries. Traceable change.**
